## Root mistake

Expire-on-no-follow-through / aged-call-watch expire was added for the **site** watch UI. The **server watch store** the APIs, price-alert checker, and study routines read (`GET /api/watches`, blob `flowguard/watches-*`) never **deleted** those rows. They sat ~5 days and kept producing loser study lines.

A UI badge of `expired` is not an invalidation. If the row is still in storage, the next check / study pass treats it as active.

## Desk change that follows

1. On each **15-minute** watch check, if a watch is expired (aged call + no premium follow-through, or >1 session without +5% / historic confirm), **delete it from the armed blob**. Archive a copy under `flowguard/watches-expired-*`. Fire the webhook once as `expired`. Surface the expire alert **once**, then it is gone.
2. Also hard-expire when live (or historic last) premium is **≤−40% vs arm**, or thesis is fading **and** the watch is older than one session.
3. Do not re-upload expired ids from `localStorage`. Tombstone them on the phone so a merge cannot resurrect MMM-class rows.
4. GH-class (last still ≥+5% or historic confirmed) stays armed.

## What an MMM-class watch does on the next check

Arm ~3 Sep, call, last far below arm (or no follow-through after one session):

1. `evaluateWatch` marks `expired`.
2. `shouldDropArmedWatch` is true.
3. Server archives the row, **removes it from the armed book**, returns `removedIds`.
4. `GET /api/watches` no longer lists it. Price alerts and the study book stop treating it as active.
5. The phone drops it from `flowguard.priceWatches` and will not POST it back.

Keep the 15-minute board refresh and the UW 429 circuit breaker. Do not burn quota on extra historic calls — still one historic payload per armed watch on the check path.
